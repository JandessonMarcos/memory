What is the Memopezil price per day and per capsule?

Daily cost is the fairest way to compare any supplement, because bottle sizes and servings vary so much across brands. For Memopezil the arithmetic is simple. Divide the pack total by the number of days it covers. Every bottle is 60 capsules at two a day, so each one is 30 days.

  • 2 bottles: $158 ÷ 60 days ≈ $2.63 a day, or about $1.32 per capsule. Shipping comes on top.
  • 3 bottles: $207 ÷ 90 days = $2.30 a day, or $1.15 per capsule.
  • 6 bottles: $294 ÷ 180 days ≈ $1.63 a day, or about 82 cents per capsule.

Put that in weekly terms and it's roughly $18, $16 or $11.50. For comparison, a daily coffee shop latte costs more than any of the three. That doesn't make the supplement worth it. It just helps you place the number.

Want to run the same check on a different brand? Find the total price, the number of capsules, and the labeled serving. Capsules divided by serving gives you days. Price divided by days gives you the daily cost. It takes thirty seconds and it cuts through most of the pricing tricks in this category.

Which Memopezil package should you buy?

This is where the timeline matters more than the discount. The ingredient in Memopezil with the strongest memory evidence is bacopa monnieri, and the trials that found an effect ran 8 to 12 weeks. Does Memopezil charge for shipping?

Only on the smallest order. The 2-bottle pack lists shipping as extra, and the exact charge is shown at checkout. The 3 and 6-bottle packs ship free within the United States.

That makes the real gap between the starter pack and the 3-bottle pack a little wider than the table suggests, since the shipping charge only lands on the cheaper total. If you're outside the US, check the checkout page for what applies to your address before entering card details. Our source pages don't cover international shipping, so we won't guess at it.

Is Memopezil a subscription?

No. Each pack is a one-time charge with no auto-ship and no recurring billing. You pay once, the bottles arrive, and nothing else is charged unless you place another order yourself.

That's worth checking on any supplement you buy online, because a cheap trial that quietly turns into a monthly charge is one of the most common complaints in this market. The FTC has a short guide on buying health products online that covers what to look for. Read the order summary before you confirm, and keep the confirmation email.

How does the 60-day guarantee affect the real cost?

Every pack bought through the official checkout carries a 60-day money-back guarantee, and our review notes it applies whether bottles are full, partly used or empty. In plain terms, the money isn't final until day 60.

Here's the catch. Bacopa's evidence window is 8 to 12 weeks, and 60 days is about 8.5 weeks. If you want both a fair trial and a live refund option, you have to decide right at the boundary. Start the first bottle the day it arrives and set a reminder for week eight. The official terms page sets out how to claim, so read it the day you order.

Bought from a marketplace or a reseller? Then the guarantee doesn't apply at all. That alone can make a cheaper listing far more expensive if the product doesn't suit you.

Why is Memopezil not on Amazon, and are cheaper listings safe?

Memopezil is sold only through its official checkout. It isn't stocked by Amazon, Walmart, CVS or GNC. Any listing you find on a marketplace is either a reseller or a counterfeit, and neither is covered by the guarantee.

A lower price on an unofficial site should make you more careful, not less. You can't check where the bottles came from, how they were stored, or whether the capsules match the label. With supplements, that's a real risk. If a listing looks too cheap, it probably isn't the same product.

How does Memopezil's price compare with other memory supplements?

Comparing on bottle price is misleading, because servings differ so much. Some products are one capsule a day, some are two or three, and some bottles last 30 days while others last 45. Always convert to cost per day before you compare.

Price also isn't the only line that differs. Products like Prevagen and Neuriva are sold through stores, so their refund terms depend on which retailer you bought from. Memopezil's 60-day guarantee comes from the manufacturer and travels with every official order. Our pages on Prevagen alternatives and the rest of the category walk through those differences.

Then there's what you get for the money. A cheaper product with a hidden proprietary blend might be good value or might be mostly filler, and you can't tell. A more expensive one with every dose printed at least lets you check. Read the label before you read the price tag.

Is Memopezil worth the price?

That depends on what you're paying for. Three of its five ingredients, bacopa, rhodiola and L-theanine, are at or near amounts used in research. Two, panax ginseng and the BCAAs, are well below studied doses. Our ingredient-by-ingredient guide covers each one. So part of every dollar goes to ingredients that are there more for the label than for the effect.

On the other side, every dose is printed. That's rarer than it should be, and it means you know what you're buying. Compared with the other formulas in our 2026 memory supplement rankings, $1.63 to $2.63 a day sits in the normal range for a multi-ingredient product with a real guarantee.

What the price doesn't buy is certainty. No clinical trial has tested the finished formula. Evidence for bacopa is not evidence for this bottle. And if your memory changes are sudden or getting worse, that's a doctor's appointment first, not a supplement order.

There's also a cost that never appears on the checkout page: your time. Twelve weeks of taking two capsules every morning is a real commitment. If the habits that matter most for memory, steady sleep, regular walking and treating things like high blood pressure, aren't in place yet, they'll do more for the same twelve weeks than any capsule. A supplement is an add-on to those, not a substitute. Spend the money once the basics are handled, and you'll also have a cleaner read on whether the product is doing anything.

Who should not spend the money

  • Anyone on blood thinners, diabetes or thyroid medication, antidepressants or sedatives who hasn't checked with their doctor. Ginseng and rhodiola both interact.
  • People who are pregnant or breastfeeding.
  • Anyone wanting a same-day effect. This is a slow, cumulative formula.
  • Anyone who'd only ever buy one or two bottles. You'd stop before the evidence window opens.
