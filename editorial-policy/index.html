Editorial Policy

How this site is written, who writes it, and what we will not do. Published in full so you can hold us to it.

The short version

  • Written by a small independent team. Not written by doctors, not reviewed by one before publication.
  • Claims are sourced to public health bodies and published research, and we say when evidence is weak.
  • Some links earn us a commission. That never changes a ranking or a conclusion.
  • We do not invent doses, reviews, ratings, credentials or study results.

Who writes this

Memory Lab is produced by a small independent team. We are not physicians, and until September 2026 this site carried bylines implying a named medical reviewer checked every article. That was not accurate, and we removed it. Articles now carry an editorial byline, which is what they always should have carried.

We think the honest version is more useful to you anyway. You can judge a summary of published research on whether it cites its sources and admits what it does not know. You cannot judge a credential you have no way to verify.

Where our claims come from

Our sources are public and named at the bottom of every article. In practice that usually means the National Institute on Aging, the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements, the National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health, MedlinePlus, Harvard Health, and the primary literature where a specific trial matters.

Where the research disagrees with itself, we say so instead of picking the flattering side. Panax ginseng for cognition is a good example: the trials genuinely do not agree, and any page telling you otherwise is selling something.

How we write about supplements

  • Ingredient evidence is not product evidence. A trial showing bacopa works at 300mg of a standardised extract tells you nothing about how much is in a given capsule.
  • If a label does not disclose amounts, we report that it does not. We never estimate a dose and present it as fact.
  • We publish the timescale. Bacopa needs 8 to 12 weeks in the studies. Saying so costs us sales and is the only honest answer.
  • Interactions and exclusions get their own section, not a footnote. Warfarin, diabetes medication, pregnancy and surgery are named explicitly.
  • No disease claims. Nothing on this site treats, prevents or reverses dementia, and we will not imply that it does.

Affiliate links

We are reader-supported. Some links earn us a commission at no extra cost to you, and every page carries that disclosure. Rankings are not sold, and a company cannot pay to be listed or to change what we say about it. When we recommend something, the criteria are published alongside the recommendation so you can disagree with our reasoning rather than take our word for it.

What we will not do

  • Invent authors, credentials or medical reviewers.
  • Publish fabricated customer reviews, star ratings, testimonials or sales figures.
  • Use fake scarcity, countdowns or "only a few left" pressure.
  • Cite a study that does not exist, or describe a real study as finding something it did not.
  • Suggest that any supplement is an alternative to seeing a doctor.

Corrections

If something here is wrong, tell us and we will fix it and say what changed. That includes this page. The medical-reviewer byline came down because it was indefensible, and leaving a record of that is part of the point.

Medical disclaimer

This site is educational and is not medical advice. It cannot diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any condition. Memory changes that you or the people around you have actually noticed deserve a proper assessment, and several common causes are treatable once identified, including thyroid problems, B12 deficiency, sleep apnoea, depression and a range of ordinary medications. Speak to a doctor before starting any supplement, particularly if you take prescription medication.
