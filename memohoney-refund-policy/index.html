How does the MemoHoney refund work, step by step?

Here's the process as described in the terms covered in our MemoHoney review. Read the current version on the official site before you order, since terms can change.

  • Step 1: Buy from the official site. The guarantee is the seller's own, so it's tied to an official order. Keep the confirmation email.
  • Step 2: Note the purchase date. The 60 days run from purchase. Put the deadline in your calendar the day you order.
  • Step 3: Use the product. Some versions of the terms ask that you take it for at least 30 days before requesting a refund. Start on the day it arrives.
  • Step 4: Email the seller to request a refund. The request goes by email, not through a portal. Include your order number and the name and email you ordered with.
  • Step 5: Follow the return instructions you're given. Use the address and any reference the seller provides in its reply rather than one you found elsewhere.
  • Step 6: Pack every bottle, including the empty ones, and ship them back at your own cost. Use a service with tracking.
  • Step 7: Wait for processing. Allow roughly 5 to 10 business days after the seller receives the package, then check your statement.

Seven steps, and none of them is hard. The ones people miss are step 2 and step 6: they lose track of the date, or they've already thrown out the empty bottles.

What does the 60-day guarantee actually promise?

It promises a refund if you're not satisfied, requested within 60 days of purchase, on the conditions above. That's the core.

A few details aren't covered by the terms we reviewed, and they matter enough to check on the official refund page before you buy:

  • whether the 60 days means your email must be sent by day 60, or the bottles must arrive by then
  • whether the original $9.99 shipping on a 2-bottle order is refunded along with the product
  • whether the refund goes back to your original card or another way
  • what reference, if any, you need to write on the parcel

We won't guess at those. Where the terms are silent, plan for the stricter reading: assume the bottles need to be back with the seller inside the 60 days, and assume shipping costs aren't refunded.

What should the refund request email say?

Keep it short and factual. The goal is a dated, written request that gives the seller everything they need to reply with return instructions in one go. Something like this works:

  • Subject: Refund request, order [your order number]
  • Opening line: "I'd like to request a refund under the 60-day guarantee for the order above, placed on [date]."
  • Details: the full name, email and shipping address used on the order, and how many bottles you'll be returning.
  • The ask: "Please send the return address and any reference I should include on the parcel."
  • Close: your name and a phone number, in case they prefer to confirm by phone.

You don't need to justify the request or explain why the product didn't suit you, though a sentence is fine if you want to give feedback. Send it from the same email address you ordered with, so the seller can match it to the order easily. Save the sent message, and don't ship anything until you have the seller's reply with the return details.

How long does a MemoHoney refund take?

Add up three stretches of time. First, the seller's reply to your email. Second, the return parcel's trip, which depends on the service you choose and where you're shipping from. Third, processing, which the terms put at roughly 5 to 10 business days after the seller receives the package.

In practice, from your first email to money back in your account can easily run two to four weeks. That's why requesting on day 58 is risky. If the terms turn out to count the bottles' arrival rather than your email, a late request may not make it.

While you wait, keep the tracking page bookmarked and note the delivery date when it appears. That date is when the processing clock starts. If ten business days pass after delivery with no refund and no message, reply in the same email thread with the tracking number. Starting a fresh conversation tends to slow things down, because the seller has to find your order again.

Is 60 days enough time to judge MemoHoney?

Not fully, and it's worth being clear about that before you buy. The formula has two halves on different clocks. The nitric oxide amino acids act within hours if at all, so 60 days is plenty for them. Ginkgo and bacopa are cumulative. Bacopa's trials report differences after 8 to 12 weeks (our bacopa monnieri guide explains why it's so slow), and 60 days is about 8.5 weeks.

How to make the week-seven call

Seven weeks is early for the botanicals, so don't expect a dramatic answer. Look for direction instead. Has your retest score moved at all? Do your weekly notes show fewer of the slips that bothered you at the start? Are you sleeping and eating the same, so that any change could plausibly be the capsule?

If the honest answer to all three is no, requesting the refund is reasonable. If there's a small but real shift, you might decide the remaining weeks are worth the risk of losing the refund. Either choice is fine. What doesn't work is drifting past day 60 without deciding.

What does using the refund cost you?

The return postage, mainly. On a 2-bottle order that's a small parcel. On six bottles it's heavier, and it comes out of your own pocket. If the original shipping charge isn't refunded (check the terms), the 2-bottle buyer also loses that $9.99.

There's also a time cost: an email, a trip to the post office and a few weeks of waiting. It's not much. Still, it's worth knowing that this isn't a one-click process, and that larger orders mean a larger return. If you think a refund is genuinely likely, a 3-bottle order keeps the exit cheaper than six while still covering a proper trial. What are the most common refund mistakes?

  • Buying from a reseller. The seller can't refund an order it has no record of.
  • Counting from delivery. The clock starts at purchase. Shipping time eats into it.
  • Recycling the empties. The terms ask for every bottle back.
  • Starting late. Leaving the bottles in a drawer for two weeks means you'll decide with even less data.
  • Waiting until the last week. Email replies and parcel transit take time.
  • Sending without tracking. If the parcel goes missing, you have no proof it was returned.
  • Assuming "no questions asked". The process has conditions. Read them once, at the start.

Should the guarantee be your reason to buy?

No. A guarantee lowers the cost of being wrong, but it doesn't make the product right for you. Decide on the merits first: the once-daily routine, the blood-flow approach, the seven printed doses, and the fact that ginkgo and bacopa appear without amounts. No published trial has tested the finished formula, and ingredient evidence isn't product evidence.

Then check the safety side. If you take blood thinners, blood pressure medication, nitrates or PDE5 inhibitors, have a bleeding disorder or surgery coming up, or are pregnant or breastfeeding, talk to your doctor before starting. A refund can't undo a bad interaction. And if your memory has changed suddenly or keeps getting worse, that calls for a medical appointment, not a 60-day trial.
