What does that come to per year?

Before committing to either, it's worth knowing whether the gummy does anything for you, which is what the guarantee window is for.

What does it cost per milligram?

Here's a less flattering way to read the same numbers. Each gummy carries 82 mg of actives. At $2.63 a day you're paying about 3.2 cents per milligram of blend. At $1.63 it's about 2 cents. That number isn't meaningful on its own, but it explains why single-ingredient powders look so cheap by comparison: they sell grams, and this sells milligrams wrapped in a gummy.

Is there a Vigor Boost subscription?

No subscription or autoship appears on the official order page. Each package is billed once. That's worth pointing out because surprise recurring charges are one of the most common complaints in the men's supplement market, and many "free trial" offers quietly enroll buyers in monthly shipments.

Still, read the checkout page before you submit. Look for any pre-ticked box offering extra bottles or add-on products, and check that the total matches the package you chose. Your bank statement should show one charge for the order.

Other costs to watch for

  • Shipping on the 2-bottle pack. It's added at checkout, so the true daily cost is a bit above $2.63.
  • Upsell screens. Some checkouts offer extra products after payment. Accept only what you actually want.
  • Return shipping on a refund. If the refund terms ask you to send bottles back at your own cost, that comes out of what you recover.
  • Currency and card fees if you order from outside the US.

None of these is unusual for direct-to-consumer supplements. They're simply easier to handle when you know they're coming, and they change which pack looks cheapest once everything is added up.

Which Vigor Boost pack should you buy first?

It depends on how sure you are, and for a first order, most people shouldn't be very sure. There's no clinical trial on the gummy, and the 82 mg blend is small compared with research doses of its ingredients. Our does Vigor Boost work page walks through that evidence. With that in mind:

  • 2 bottles covers the full 60-day guarantee window at the lowest upfront cost. It's the most cautious choice, but the most expensive per day.
  • 3 bottles gives 90 days at a lower daily price with free shipping. You'd decide within the first 60 days whether to keep going, with a month in reserve.
  • 6 bottles makes sense for someone who has already taken Vigor Boost, liked it and plans to continue. As a first order, it's the biggest bet on an untested product.

For most first-time buyers, the 3-bottle pack is the reasonable middle. It lowers the daily cost noticeably without committing you to six months of a supplement you haven't tried.

There's also a group for whom the right pack is none. If you take nitrates, ED medication or blood pressure drugs, have thyroid or liver disease, or are very sensitive to caffeine, get your doctor's view before spending anything. How does the 60-day guarantee work with each pack?

Orders through the official checkout come with a 60-day money-back guarantee. The timing matters more than the headline:

  • With 2 bottles, the guarantee covers almost the entire supply.
  • With 3 bottles, you'll have about a month's supply left when the window closes.
  • With 6 bottles, the window ends when you're only a third of the way through.

Read the current refund terms on the official site before ordering. Check whether you need to return bottles, including opened ones, who pays return shipping and how to start a request. Keep your order confirmation email, note your delivery date and set a calendar reminder for about day 45. Decide by then, so you're not racing the deadline.

One practical tip: don't throw away empty bottles until the window has closed. Some refund policies ask for them back, and you can't return what you've recycled. Keep the outer box and packing slip too, since they make it easy to match the return to your order.

How to use the window well

Pick two or three things to rate weekly, such as afternoon energy and interest in sex, and write the scores down from the first day. If nothing has moved by week six, request the refund. Writing it down protects you from the sunk-cost feeling that makes people keep taking something because they've already paid for it.

Where to buy Vigor Boost: is it on Amazon?

Vigor Boost is sold through its official website. We'd buy it there and only there, for three reasons.

What are you paying for in each gummy?

This is the question most price pages skip. Each gummy contains an 82 mg proprietary blend of nine ingredients: muira puama, ashwagandha, maca, catuaba, green tea extract, caffeine (10 mg), L-arginine, tribulus and horny goat weed. The label says the blend is equivalent to about 567 mg of dry powders, which describes the plant material used to make the extracts, not the amount you swallow.

At $1.63 to $2.63 a day, you're paying for convenience and a mix of traditional botanicals, not for research-level doses. Plain maca powder or a standardized ashwagandha capsule with a printed dose usually costs less per day and delivers the amounts used in studies. What you give up is the single daily gummy and the multi-herb mix. Our Vigor Boost ingredients breakdown shows how little room each herb has in the blend.

Is Vigor Boost worth the price?

It depends on what you're buying it for. As a pleasant daily habit with familiar botanicals and a mild hint of caffeine, $1.63 to $2.63 a day is in the normal range for branded gummies. As a way to get proven doses of maca or ashwagandha, it's poor value, because the blend can't hold them.

Put another way, the same $207 for a 3-bottle order could buy a few months of plain maca powder, a basic set of dumbbells or most of a year of a budget gym. Those aren't either-or choices, and plenty of men do all of them. But if the budget is tight, the habits with the best evidence for energy and sexual health are exercise, sleep, less alcohol and a healthy weight. A gummy is the optional extra, not the foundation.

And if the reason you're shopping is a change in erections, the most valuable money you can spend is on a doctor's visit. New or persistent erection problems can be an early sign of heart disease, diabetes or low testosterone, and those are treatable. A checkup costs about the same as a 3-bottle order and tells you far more. More on that in our men's health section.

Low energy plus a fading sex drive is also a classic pattern of low testosterone, which a simple morning blood test can check. Our guide to low testosterone in men lists the symptoms that justify asking for one. If the result is normal, you've ruled something out for the price of a lab visit, and you can try a gummy with clearer expectations.
