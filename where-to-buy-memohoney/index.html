How to confirm you're on the official order page

Lookalike pages copy logos and photos easily. They're worse at copying the details. A quick routine before you pay:

  • Read the full domain in the address bar, letter by letter, not just the logo on the page.
  • Check that the packages match what the seller publishes: 2, 3 or 6 bottles, with the bigger two shipping free in the US.
  • Look for the refund terms and contact details before checkout, not after.
  • Make sure the order summary says one-time charge and shows the total, shipping included.
  • Pay by credit card if you can. It gives you a dispute route if something goes wrong.

None of this takes long. It's the same habit that protects you on any site selling health products, and it's worth more than any discount code.

Capsules themselves are harder to judge. Uneven size, a strange smell, clumped powder or capsules stuck together suggest moisture damage or poor manufacturing. If the bottle passes every check above but the capsules look off, contact the seller before taking any.

Why supplements get faked at all

Supplements are an easy target. A capsule of powder costs little to produce, labels are simple to print, and most buyers can't test what's inside. Popular direct-sold brands are especially exposed because shoppers search for them by name on marketplaces where anyone can list a product.

The FDA has warned for years about products sold as supplements that turn out to contain undeclared ingredients, sometimes including prescription drugs. That's the real risk with a fake. It isn't just wasted money. You may be swallowing something that isn't on the label at all, which matters even more if you take other medication.

What are the warning signs on a fake MemoHoney website?

Counterfeit risk isn't only about bottles. Lookalike sales pages are a bigger problem for direct-sold products, because a page is cheaper to copy than a capsule.

  • The domain is off by a letter or two, uses an odd ending, or doesn't match the one you clicked through to.
  • There's a countdown timer or "only 3 left" pressure. Scarcity tricks are a reason to slow down, not speed up.
  • It offers a free bottle if you just pay shipping. That model usually hides a recurring charge.
  • It promises a cure for dementia, Alzheimer's or any disease. No supplement can legally or honestly make that claim.
  • Checkout asks for unusual payment, like a bank transfer, crypto or a gift card.
  • There's no clear refund page, no contact details and no named company behind the product.

The real product, for reference, is described as having a named distributor in the United States, a working support line and a published refund policy. If you can't find those, you're probably not on the official page.

What should you do if you think you bought a fake?

Stop taking it. Then work through these steps:

  • Keep everything. Bottle, packaging, receipt, the listing URL and screenshots of the seller page.
  • Ask the platform for a refund. Marketplaces and card issuers usually have a process for items not as described.
  • Contact the official seller with photos of the label and lot number. They can tell you whether that lot exists.
  • Report problems. A health reaction can be reported to the FDA through MedWatch. A deceptive seller can be reported to the FTC.
  • See a doctor if you feel unwell. Bring the bottle so they can see what you took.

How should you store MemoHoney and check the expiry?

Once you have a genuine bottle, keeping it in good shape is mostly common sense. Follow whatever the label says first. In general, for capsule supplements:

  • Keep it cool and dry. A kitchen cupboard away from the stove is better than the bathroom, where shower steam raises humidity.
  • Close the cap tightly after each use, and leave any desiccant packet inside.
  • Don't leave it in a car. Summer heat in a parked car can damage capsules and their contents.
  • Check the expiry date on arrival. On a six-bottle order, the last bottle should still be in date six months from now.
  • Don't use it past the date, or if capsules have changed color, smell or texture.
  • Keep it out of reach of children. Supplements are still products with doses.

The expiry check matters more with bigger orders. If you buy six bottles, open them in order and glance at the dates before you put them away. Any bottle that won't last until you reach it is worth raising with the seller straight away, while you're inside the guarantee window.

Taking it with you when you travel

A month's supply is small, which makes travel easy. Carry the capsules in the original bottle when you can, so the label and lot number stay with them. If you use a weekly pill organizer, keep the bottle at home so you can still check the expiry and the panel. In hot weather, pack it in hand luggage rather than a bag that sits in a car trunk or an airport cart in the sun.
